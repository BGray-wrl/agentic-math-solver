## Recommended for the paper (pick 2–3)

The paper is short and makes one simple claim, so the figures should not
out-number the argument.

- **Fig 7 — headline.** Forest plot of pass-agreement 95% CIs with the cost of
  each system alongside. The absolute pass-agreement numbers read more tangibly
  than a cost-vs-accuracy scatter, and the boxed frontier costs ($32.45, $28.61)
  next to the sub-$1 cheap judges make the ~100× gap immediate. Preferred over
  Fig 1, which is busier.
- **Fig 4 — recommended second.** Directly supports the abstract's secondary
  claim that consensus rules (esp. all-three-pass) are the *most stable*; pairs
  with the run-to-run §6.2.
- **Fig 3 — recommended third** for the full-benchmark §6.1: the precision/recall
  "dial" is the most actionable practitioner takeaway.

Fig 1 (cost-vs-accuracy scatter) is the interchangeable alternative headline to
Fig 7. **Fig 2** is best held for the appendix, alongside a fuller treatment of
the rank-correlation result. Figs 5 and 6 are supporting/explanatory (offsetting
biases; leader sample-dependence) — good appendix material or §6.1 inset.

### Fig 3: precision/recall + iso-F1 vs. an ROC plot

A reviewer may ask why we use a PR plane rather than ROC. Brief justification:

- **Each system is one fixed operating point, not a swept threshold.** An ROC
  curve is traced by sweeping a continuous decision threshold to produce a family
  of (FPR, TPR) pairs. Our judges emit a discrete pass/fail vote with no tunable
  cutoff, so each system is a single point — there is no curve to draw per system.
- **The contours do for F1 what ROC iso-lines do for cost.** Each dashed iso-F1
  curve is the locus of (precision, recall) pairs with constant F1; a system's
  F1 is read off by which contour it sits on, analogous to reading performance
  off ROC iso-cost lines.
- **The consensus rules are the "sweep."** Moving majority → unanimous-pair →
  all-three-pass walks a discrete family of operating points up the
  precision/recall trade-off — the role a threshold sweep plays in ROC, but
  realised by changing the voting rule instead of a cutoff.
- **PR is the right plane under class imbalance.** Only ~30% of instances are
  human-pass. ROC's FPR is diluted by the large negative class and can look
  deceptively strong; precision conditions on *predicted* passes and is exactly
  the quantity a practitioner gating on "pass" cares about.
